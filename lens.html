EXPERIMENT 05 / HOW A LENS BLURS LIGHT

Throughthe lens.

Rows of street lamps and a railing of small lights, from half a metre to six hundred metres away, drawn through a camera lens: every light becomes the aperture's shape at its own blur while the focus walks out and back. Change the aperture, the f-number and the focal length below. The light starts unconserved, the way a cheap blur draws it: switch conserving light on to see the street as a lens would.

THE PAGE IN FRONT / THE STREET BEHIND IT05 — A THIN LENS, AN APERTURE, A FOCUS

WHERE THE SHAPES COME FROM

One lens,
three numbers.

01 / BLUR

Set by the lens.

A thin lens sends each point it is not focused on to a disc. The disc grows with the aperture, which is the focal length over the f-number, and with how far the point is from the focus. A long lens wide open blurs the most; stopped down to f/8 most of the street comes sharp.

02 / SHAPE

The shape is the aperture.

Each disc is the aperture itself, drawn at its blur: six straight blades make hexagons, a soap-bubble lens a bright rim, a mirror lens a ring, and an old lens vignetted at its edges cat's eyes that turn round the centre of the frame.

03 / LIGHT

Blur keeps the light.

A blurred lamp spreads the same light over a wider disc, so it is dimmer per pixel and no less bright in all. Switch that off and every disc keeps the brightness it had in focus: the street turns to glare, the usual mistake of a blur drawn as a sprite.

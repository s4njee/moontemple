EXPERIMENT 08 / A PATH ACROSS THE SEA

Moonlitwater.

A low full moon over a breezy sea, through an 85 mm lens at f/1.8. Every ripple that tilts the moon's reflection towards you is a glint at its own distance, so the moon's path is a wide river of them. The focus sweeps slowly out to sea and back: close, the glints blur into soft discs; far, they sharpen into sparkle.

WHERE THE GLITTER COMES FROM

A thousand
small mirrors.

01 / PATH

Waves tilted just so.

A flat sea would show one reflection of the moon. A moving one is covered in small facets at every angle, and wherever a facet is tilted to send the moon to your eye, it glints. Those facets lie along a path from the moon to you, wider the rougher the sea.

02 / DISCS

Each glint at its distance.

Every glint is a point of light somewhere on the sea, from a few metres out to the horizon. Focused close, the far ones blur into discs the shape of the aperture, so the path becomes a river of discs; focused out to sea, they shrink back to sparkle.

03 / TWINKLE

Never a flash.

A facet tilts into place and out again as the wave passes, so each glint lights and fades. Their twinkling is capped at four a second, and they are many and small, so the path shimmers without ever flashing.

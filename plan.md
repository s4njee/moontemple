# Moon Temple — WebGPU bokeh and lens-light effects research site

A research site that implements, measures and documents effects made of light seen through a lens: out-of-focus orbs, depth of field, halos, glints, flares and night haze. It asks one question of each: **would you actually ship this on a website?** Every effect gets a live demo, readable source, a write-up and a practicality scorecard. Sibling of [Fire Temple](../firetemple/plan.md) and [Storm Temple](../stormtemple/plan.md), on the same page structure and the shared temple core.

The reference picture is a dark night scene. One bright source with a wide violet halo sits above a field of soft blue-violet discs at several depths, some overlapping, all drifting. Two things make it read as a photograph rather than as circles: each disc is the shape of the lens's aperture, and each conserves the light of the point it came from.

## Goals

- Cover the optics behind the look: the thin-lens circle of confusion, aperture shapes and their aberrations, energy-conserving point sources, diffraction glare, lens flare ghosts, halation, and light scattered by haze.
- Compare the real-time depth-of-field methods on one scene against a brute-force reference, for cost and error: gather, separable hexagonal and circular kernels, sprite scatter, and layer blur with no depth buffer.
- Ship effects shaped like website parts, not only full-screen scenes: bokeh heroes, a focus pull on scroll, glints on hover, a flare that follows the pointer, a loader that comes into focus.
- Say plainly when CSS (radial gradients, `filter: blur()`) or a looped video does the job as well as a shader.
- Build the optics once, shaped to move into `@mk7s/hologpu-temple-core`, so Eightfold Path's tilt-shift and bokeh stories (5.1, 5.2) and Light Temple's fireflies (E7-S2) can use them rather than build them again.

## Non-goals

- A camera simulator. Optics are in scope where they change what a visitor sees; ray-tracing a lens prescription is in scope only as the reference for a cheap approximation (story 2.7).
- Depth of field over live page content. A canvas cannot read the page's pixels (Fire Temple's [backdrop strategy](../firetemple/docs/backdrop-strategy.md)); defocusing the page means CSS, re-rendered content or images the page hands over (story 6.1).
- Photographic accuracy for its own sake. A plausible, stable, cheap disc beats a correct one that shimmers.
- Space subjects (stars, planets, moon phases as data). Those are research.md's Space family. Moon Temple keeps the moon as a light source seen through air and glass: its halo, corona and glitter path.

## Carried over from the other temples

Findings that shape this plan rather than being rediscovered.

- **Per-pass GPU timings are indicative only** (Fire Temple). Passes overlap on tile-based GPUs, the browser floor is about 1 ms on the development machine, and effects under about 2 ms cannot be ranked against each other. Cost studies run on Dawn under Node, and Chrome's own frame reading decides the tier.
- **A canvas cannot read the page** (Water and Fire Temple). Element geometry is available every frame (`src/gpu/dom-rects.ts`); element pixels are not, except an image or canvas the page owns, uploaded with `copyExternalImageToTexture`.
- **A baked noise texture beats procedural noise per pixel** (Fire Temple Epic 4). It moved six effects from Tier B to A. The same is likely for hashed orb fields at high density.
- **Bloom's level count must follow the canvas size** (Fire Temple 3.4), and a Karis average on the first downsample stops single bright pixels from flickering (Light Temple E1-S2). Both matter more here, where every highlight is a single bright pixel before it is defocused.
- **A frozen frame can be the hazard** (Storm Temple 6.2). A paused effect shows one frame for as long as the visitor looks. A flare or a glint at full brightness must not be the held frame.
- **Quick taps slip between frames** (Storm Temple's pages). An effect that reads a press once a frame misses a tap whose down and up land in the same frame; the page holds the release for two frames.
- **Stable glitter** (Water Temple `sun-glint`). Hashed cells that twinkle with the surface and stay put under camera motion. Glints here start from it.
- **Spectral optics exist** (Light Temple `holo-card`, `src/shaders/optics.wgsl`). The grating equation and `spectral_rgb` cover the diffraction colour in stories 2.4, 2.6 and 5.2.
- **Scratch work needs a home.** Long sweeps and probes go in `experiments/`, outside test, typecheck and lint.

## Findings so far

- **Generated discs beat per-pixel orbs.** `bokeh-drift` draws its orbs as one instanced quad each and costs about what the HDR tail does (0.4 to 0.6 ms at 1080p on Dawn, M1 Max), from 50 to 440 orbs. `hello`, which finds its orbs per pixel in a hashed grid (27 lookups a pixel), costs 1.44 ms. Fire Temple found the same of procedural noise per pixel.
- **Conservation needs the exact small case.** Above about three pixels a one-pixel edge divided by the shape's area plus Steiner's π/12 keeps a point's light within 2%; below it only the exact area of the pixel inside the disc does. Combining two clips by multiplying their edges squares the ramp and loses 0.33/r of the light; take the minimum.
- **Big bokeh needs close focus.** A 50 mm f/1.8 focused at 6 m blurs infinity to about 3 px at 700 px tall. The reference look is a lens focused within a metre or so, with the lights far behind it, which is also why its discs are all about the same size.
- **A sharp image cannot give the foreground back.** In `dof-lab`, the separable hexagon gets within 10.7 levels RMS of the ray-traced reference with the subject in focus but 15.3 with the background in focus, because a blurred foreground should spread over and show what is behind it, and a post-process given one sharp image has neither.
- **Points built on the CPU are cheap until they are not.** Hundreds to a few thousand points a frame cost 0.1 to 0.5 ms of main-thread time and nothing measurable on the GPU. `glitter`'s 24 000 facet tests cost 2.7 ms of CPU against 0.5 ms of GPU: past about ten thousand, the test belongs in a shader.
- **Uniforms written between passes in one command buffer all arrive with the last value.** `dof-lab`'s held frame encoded 128 reference batches before one submit, each writing its batch number with `queue.writeBuffer`; every batch ran with the last number, so the frame held 8 rays' worth of light and said it held 1 024. Per-pass values now come from the draw's first instance. The headless tests submitted batch by batch and could not see it; `tests/dof.test.ts` now encodes them together too.
- **Bound thin things tightly.** The glare layer's first version drew each light as one square big enough for its longest spike, most of a frame tall, and evaluated every spike there in three colours: 4.78 ms for `lens-light` at 1080p. One quad per ray, a few pixels wide, does the same work in 0.52 ms.
- **Real airlight is wide.** The glow of a light in fog falls off about as 1 / angle, so it lifts the whole frame long before it looks like a halo; the stand-in it replaced was too tight.

## Practicality tiers

Every effect is tagged with a tier. Budgets are starting targets to be validated in Epic 8.

| Tier            | Meaning                                             | Shape                                              | GPU budget @1080p                   |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **A** Drop-in   | Safe as ambient decoration, including mobile        | One or two fragment passes, no compute             | ≤ 2 ms on integrated GPU            |
| **B** Feature   | Hero sections and interactive set pieces            | Multi-pass, a depth-of-field pass or small compute | ≤ 6 ms on integrated GPU            |
| **C** Showpiece | Dedicated page or landing experience, desktop-first | Full-resolution scatter, FFT glare, a 3D scene     | ≤ 12 ms on discrete / Apple Silicon |

Budgets include the HDR tail. Depth of field is the stage most likely to push an effect up a tier, so its cost is a baseline row in every study, as the tail is.

## Architecture sketch

```
moontemple/
  index.html
  vite.config.ts
  src/
    main.ts            bootstrap: one shared GPU session, mounts an effect
    gpu/               device, canvas, frame loop, shaders, uniforms, targets, passes, post/
                       (bloom, tonemap), strokes, dom-rects, pointer, scroll, plus
                       optics/ (lens model, circle of confusion, aperture kernels),
                       dof/ (reference scatter, gather, separable, sprite scatter, layers),
                       points/ (energy-conserving point sources, glints) and
                       glare/ (spikes, FFT glare, flare ghosts, halation)
    wgsl/              shared chunks: hash, noise, sdf, colour, plus aperture, coc, points,
                       spectral and scatter (fog in-scattering)
    effects/<id>/      meta.ts (eager), index.ts + *.wgsl (lazy chunk), notes.md, poster.webp
    shell/             app frame, gallery, effect page, contexts, controls, notes, scorecard, HUD
    lib/               the library entry: mount an effect on a caller's canvas
  tests/               vitest on Dawn's Node bindings: real WebGPU, no browser
  experiments/         scratch sweeps and probes; not part of the test run
  docs/                research output: support matrix delta, cost studies, method comparison
```

Effect contract, unchanged from Storm Temple so effects and shell code move between the temples:

```ts
// src/effects/<id>/index.ts exports `effect`; meta.ts exports `meta`.
export interface EffectModule<S extends ParamSchema> {
  meta: EffectMeta; // id, title, epic, tier, tags, summary, scorecard, layout
  hdr?: boolean; // asks for the extended-range canvas where allowed
  params: S; // drives controls, docs table, URL state
  presets?: Record<string, Partial<ParamValues<S>>>;
  create(ctx: EffectContext<S>): Promise<EffectInstance>;
}

export interface EffectInstance {
  resize(width: number, height: number, dpr: number): void;
  frame(time: FrameTime, encoder: GPUCommandEncoder, target: GPUTextureView): void; // time.still: the held frame
  stats?(): Record<string, string | number>;
  destroy(): void;
}
```

The full types live in `src/effects/types.ts`. `tests/flash.test.ts` and `tests/still.test.ts` run over every registered effect and preset, so a new effect is covered as soon as its directory exists.

## Definition of done (per effect story)

1. Runs inside its tier budget on the reference devices, with numbers recorded. Until the device matrix exists (8.2) the only reference device is the development machine, an M1 Max: measured per frame on Dawn and as one frame in Chrome, taking the lowest tier whose budget neither number exceeds. An A or B label is provisional until 8.2, and the scorecard says so.
2. Parameters exposed through the schema, with at least two presets.
3. Handles resize, DPR changes, device loss, offscreen pause and `prefers-reduced-motion`. The held frame shows the subject, never a flare or glint frozen at its brightest (`tests/still.test.ts`).
4. Stays under the flash threshold (WCAG 2.3.1) at default settings and in every preset not declared in `scorecard.flashPresets`.
5. Conserves light where it defocuses: a point's total light is the same in focus and out of it, within 2%, checked numerically for every chunk that spreads a point.
6. Shown in at least one realistic web context, over both a dark and a light page and at phone size, and its screenshots looked at.
7. `notes.md` written: how it works, cost, pitfalls, when to use it, references. Where a CSS version exists, the notes say how close it gets.
8. Scorecard filled in and fallback poster captured.

## Epic 2 — Optics building blocks

Shared WGSL and TypeScript that every later effect uses, each proven in a small test scene with numeric tests. Shaped from the start to move into the temple core (7.6).

- [x] **2.1 Thin-lens model** [—] (S) — circle of confusion from focal length, f-number, focus distance and depth, signed (near negative, far positive) and in pixels for a given sensor size and canvas. A "photographer's" parameter set (focal length, f-stop, focus distance) and an "artist's" one (focus depth, blur at infinity), both mapping to the same internal model. Scene: `lens-model`, a row of points walking through focus with the CoC plotted. _`src/gpu/optics/lens.ts` and `src/wgsl/coc.wgsl`: the whole model is two numbers, the focus distance and the radius of a point at infinity, `radius = blur at infinity × (1 − focus / depth)`, negative in front of focus. `lensFromCamera` maps focal length, f-number, focus and a full-frame sensor onto it (`tests/lens.test.ts` checks a 50 mm f/1.8 against the textbook disc). `lens-model` is a night street instead of a plot: two rows of lamps, a railing and a city, with the focus walking from 0.8 to 40 m. Big bokeh needs close focus: a 50 mm at f/1.8 focused at 6 m blurs infinity to only about 3 px at 700 px tall, so the scene focuses at 1.5 m at f/1.4._
- [x] **2.2 Aperture shapes** [—] (M) — circle; n-gon with rotation and blade curvature (5 to 9 blades); and the aberrations that make lenses recognisable: cat's-eye vignetting off-axis (the swirl of a Petzval), onion rings from aspherical elements, the bright rim of over-corrected spherical aberration (soap-bubble bokeh) and the soft rim of under-correction, the donut of a mirror lens, and a custom shape from an SVG path or a texture (the heart-shaped cut-out). Each as an analytic distance function and as a baked kernel texture; compare the two on cost and edge quality. _Analytic half done in `src/wgsl/aperture.wgsl` with the shared controls in `src/gpu/optics/aperture.ts`: circle, n-gon with rotation and curvature, a rim (soap bubble or soft edge), onion rings and the cat's eye, and the near field flipped. The shape is described by its radius at each angle, so any profile in the normalised radius has the same mean over the shape as over a disc, and the shape is normalised by its area alone. Still to do: the donut, a custom shape from an SVG path or texture, and the baked-kernel comparison._ _Finished: the donut of a mirror lens (`obstruction`, its light normalised by the ring's area and the profile's mean over the ring), and any star-shaped outline baked into a 64-entry radius table (`src/gpu/optics/shapes.ts`: a heart, a star, or a traced polygon), drawn through the same light-conserving path; `tests/points.test.ts` checks them all within 2%. Baked against analytic: a 64-entry table rounds a polygon's corners by up to 2.9% of its radius (five blades; 1.4% at nine) and its area by under 1%, and since the light is normalised by the table's own area it is still conserved; the analytic polygon stays the default. Outlines with notches need 6 to 12 px before their drawn edge conserves light: below that they are drawn as the exact disc. The donut is not in the 3.1 reference._
- [x] **2.3 Energy-conserving point sources** [—] (M) — research.md's shared "point-source rendering": a point carries power, its disc's brightness is power over area, and a point smaller than a pixel is drawn with its exact pixel coverage, so a moving point neither crawls nor shimmers and its total light never changes as it moves or defocuses. Numeric test: total light constant within 2% from in focus to a 64 px disc, and from rest to moving at 0.3 px a frame. _`src/gpu/points.ts`: one instanced quad per point. Below about three pixels a pixel's share is the exact area of the pixel inside a disc (closed form from the antiderivative of a chord), which conserves the light of any point at any position; above that, the shape with a one-pixel edge, divided by its area plus Steiner's π/12 edge term. `tests/points.test.ts` sums the rendered light for six shapes, twelve radii from 0 to 32 px (in front of and behind focus) and five sub-pixel offsets: all within 2%, round within 1%. The first version multiplied the cat's-eye clip into the edge ramp even with no vignetting, squaring the ramp and losing 0.33/r of the light; intersections now take the minimum of the two coverages._
- [x] **2.4 Chromatic aberration** [—] (S) — longitudinal (purple and green fringes that swap sides through focus) and lateral (colour spread towards the edges), on discs and on the whole frame. Three spectral samples against seven against a continuous spectrum, from Light Temple's `spectral_rgb`. _In the shared point layer: `axial` gives each colour its own focus (red farther, blue nearer), `lateral` magnifies each colour about the centre, each channel drawn with its own normalised disc so each keeps its light (`tests/points.test.ts`). Three channels a pixel only when switched on. Shown in `lens-light`. The continuous-spectrum comparison is left to story 9.1._
- [x] **2.5 Halation and glow** [—] (S) — film halation (light through the emulsion reflected off the film base: a red-orange halo hugging highlights) against the tail's bloom and against a glow from a distance field. Energy-conserving: halation moves light, it does not add it. _`src/gpu/post/halation.ts`: a half-size blur of the scene blended back so each channel keeps (1 − spread) of itself and gets `spread` of its blurred self, red most (film's tint 1, 0.35, 0.05); each channel's light is unchanged to 1% (`tests/halation.test.ts`). In `lens-light` and `flare-cursor`. Not compared with a distance-field glow._
- [x] **2.6 Diffraction glare and starbursts** [—] (M) — the spikes a polygonal aperture gives a bright point (N spikes for even N blades, 2N for odd), as analytic spike sprites against the Fourier transform of the aperture image (Ritschel et al.) computed in compute, with spectral smearing of the spikes. Decide which a glint effect should use. _Analytic spikes in `src/gpu/glare/glare.ts`: n rays for an even blade count and 2n for odd, each with a Gaussian cross-section and exponential fall, whose integral is known, so they carry exactly their share (`tests/glare.test.ts`); red's run longest. One thin quad per ray: the first version's one square per light cost 4.78 ms in `lens-light` at 1080p, the quads 0.52. Spikes fade as a light's blur grows. The FFT of the aperture (Ritschel et al.) is not built; the analytic spikes are what glints use._
- [x] **2.7 Lens flare ghosts** [—] (M) — a screen-space pseudo-flare (Chapman: downsample, threshold, flip through the centre, chromatic ghosts, halo ring) against per-ghost sprites from a small lens description (a simplified Hullin et al.), plus the anamorphic streak. The sprites follow a light the effect knows; the pseudo-flare works on any image. _Sprite ghosts (`src/gpu/glare/flare.ts`): seven aperture-shaped images along the line from the light through the centre, each with its place, size and coating tint, about 4% of the light, drawn through the point layer so a hexagonal lens gives hexagonal ghosts; and an anamorphic streak from the glare layer. The screen-space pseudo-flare and a lens-prescription trace are not built._
- [x] **2.8 Light in haze** [—] (S) — the in-scattered glow of a point light in uniform fog, closed form (Sun et al.) against a short ray march; Henyey–Greenstein phase for forward scattering. This is the halo around the reference picture's light. _Closed form done: `src/gpu/optics/haze.ts` derives the single-scattering integral down to Sun et al.'s form, with the one remaining integral as a 12-point Gauss–Legendre sum, and `src/wgsl/scatter.wgsl` is its twin. Against a 200 000-step march it is within 0.14% for optical depths 0.1 to 4 and angles 0.02 to 3 radians (`tests/haze.test.ts`). `bokeh-drift` uses it. Still to do: Henyey–Greenstein forward scattering, which has no such closed form and needs the march._ _Henyey–Greenstein done, and it needed no march after all: in the closed form's substitution the light at each point turns through an angle with cosine cos 2ξ, so the phase sits inside the same one-dimensional integral, and the same 12-point rule is within 0.08% of a march for g up to 0.8 (`airlightForward`, `airlight_forward`; `tests/haze.test.ts`). `bokeh-drift` has it as `haze scatters forwards`._
- [x] **2.9 Flicker for points** [—] (S) — port Fire Temple's `flicker.wgsl` and its TypeScript twin: band-limited twinkle with the frequency and amplitude caps that keep a field of glints under the flash threshold by construction. _Ported as `src/gpu/optics/flicker.ts` and `src/wgsl/flicker.wgsl`. `tests/flicker.test.ts` checks the caps, that the worst safe settings make at most three flashes a second over 200 seeds, and that the WGSL twin gives the TypeScript's numbers. `fairy-lights` and `focus-cursor` twinkle with it._

## Epic 3 — Depth of field: the methods compared

The core research. One test scene with depth (points at many depths, an in-focus foreground edge, bright highlights behind it), every method on it, and each scored against the reference for error and cost. The scene is the `dof-lab` effect: a split view, the reference beside a method, and the difference.

- [x] **3.1 Reference scatter** [C] (M) — every pixel spreads its colour over its own circle of confusion with its aperture kernel, accumulated in compute, at low resolution, with near and far layers composited correctly. Slow on purpose; the ground truth every other method is scored against. _Built as a ray tracer instead: the shot is analytic shapes (`src/gpu/dof/scene.wgsl`), traced through the whole aperture with rays from an R2 sequence and averaged over frames: Cook et al.'s distributed ray tracing. That is the truth, where a scatter of the sharp image could not see round a blurred foreground either. Its lens radius matches the thin-lens model; one point traced through round and hexagonal lenses, in front of and behind focus, spreads to the predicted radius within 1% (`tests/dof.test.ts`). About 3.5 ms a frame at 8 rays at 1080p on the M1 Max._
- [x] **3.2 Gather, the Call of Duty way** [B] (L) — half resolution, tiles of max and min CoC with neighbourhood dilation, presorted near and far fields, a ring kernel of about 49 taps, near-field bleed over the in-focus edge (Jimenez 2014). _`src/gpu/dof/gather.ts`: half resolution, 16-pixel tiles of the largest near blur dilated 7 × 7, near and far fields on a 49-tap ring kernel turned and moved per pixel, a 4-tap tent in place of the median, and the near field over the sharp image at full resolution. Two additions: the far field under a foreground holds the background just outside it, so a see-through blurred edge shows that rather than the foreground's own sharp colour; and a spread prefilter that blurs each texel over its tap's cell, without which a small lamp in a 48-pixel blur came out as concentric rings. Lowest error or near it on every shot (`docs/studies/epic-3.md`): the default._
- [x] **3.3 Separable hexagon** [B] (M) — three skewed one-dimensional blurs in two passes give a hexagonal kernel at the cost of a separable one (White and Barré-Brisebois). _`src/gpu/dof/hexagon.ts`: two passes of 16-tap one-sided line blurs at full resolution, each tap counted only where its own circle of confusion reaches back. Against a 1 024-ray reference at 640 × 400: RMSE 10.7 and SSIM 0.915 with the subject in focus (the sharp image: 39.9, 0.647), 15.3 and 0.866 with the background in focus (32.5, 0.670). The difference is the foreground: blurred inside its own outline with a hard, opaque edge, where the reference spreads it over the background and makes it see-through. About 1.2 ms at 1080p on the M1 Max. In `dof-lab`, with the method interface in `src/gpu/dof/types.ts` for the rest of the epic._
- [x] **3.4 Circular separable convolution** [B] (M) — a disc kernel from a sum of complex Gaussian components, two passes per component (Garcia 2017); one, two and three components compared on ringing and cost. _`src/gpu/dof/circular.ts`, `circularDof(1 | 2 | 3)`: Niemitalo's fits as Garcia uses them, a horizontal pass per component into two `rgba16float` targets and one vertical pass that folds them to real. Two components: RMSE 11.5 and 14.7 against the hexagon's 12.4 and 16.0 on the night shot. One component overshoots its rim by 37% and leaves 4.6% of its light in negative lobes; three bring that to 18% and 0.4%. It costs about twice the gather and four full-resolution targets, and is recommended for no tier._
- [x] **3.5 Sprite bokeh** [B] (M) — bright pixels above a threshold appended in compute and drawn as aperture sprites with indirect draws; everything else from a cheap gather. The aperture's shape only shows on highlights, which is where the eye looks for it. _`src/gpu/dof/sprites.ts`: the excess light of each 2 × 2 block over the threshold is appended in compute (one record a block, its light-weighted centre and circle) and drawn with `drawIndirect` through the point layer's aperture, so the shape and the flip in front of focus are exact; the rest is a 48-tap gather. About 390 sprites at 640 × 400 and 2 060 at 1080p on the night shot; capped at 16 384, past which highlights fall back to the gather with no light lost. Best RMSE on the night shot with the subject in focus. Two limits: sprites draw over everything, sharp foreground included; and a photograph's lit areas, not points, make thousands of large sprites (13 ms in `photo-focus`)._
- [x] **3.6 Layer blur with no depth** [A] (M) — for flat layers, images and anything DOM-shaped: a blur per layer from its assigned depth, and the tilt-shift band (blur growing from a focus line). Compared with CSS `filter: blur()` on the same layers, timed on both sides. The method Eightfold Path's 5.1 needs. _`src/gpu/dof/layers.ts`: seven slices by circle of confusion with hat weights, each blurred at half size by its own Gaussian, composited back to front over an eighth slice that fills what a near object hid (without it the blurred rail of the edges shot came out as an opaque bar). `tilt-shift` is the same with the blur from height in the frame. Best of all at keeping a sharp foreground clean (edges shot, focus on the post), worst on light at 30 m, and the most memory of the methods (62 MB at 1080p). Still to do: the comparison with CSS `filter: blur()` on the same layers._
- [x] **3.7 Temporal stability** [—] (M) — sample jitter with and without accumulation, on a slow camera move and on twinkling highlights. A defocused highlight that flickers is worse than a sharp one. _`tests/dof-temporal.test.ts`: the camera pans a quarter of a pixel a frame with parallax (`pan` in the scene), and each method's change between frames is set against the reference's. Every method moves within 15% of the truth; the sharp image is the one that shimmers. No method jitters per frame, so accumulation had nothing to settle. Gather is the steadiest._
- [x] **3.8 Foreground and edges** [—] (S) — the classic failures, each on a dedicated shot: sharp foreground haloed by blurred background, background leaking into the foreground, a near object blurred over a sharp edge without its silhouette spilling. Which method handles which. _The `edges` shot (`scene` 2 in `scene.wgsl`; three `dof-lab` presets) and RMSE in the bands either side of each depth edge (`depthEdgeBands`). Layers and gather keep a sharp foreground cleanest; gather alone spreads a blurred foreground over a sharp edge well; sprites fail next to a sharp foreground. The table is in `docs/studies/epic-3.md`._
- [x] **3.9 Study and verdict** [—] (M) — cost at 1080p and phone resolution, error against 3.1, and texture memory for every method; the order of depth of field and bloom; a recommended default for each tier. `docs/studies/epic-3.md`. _`docs/studies/epic-3.md`, with its findings in `epic-3.findings.md`, which `npm run study` now keeps. Gather is the default for Tier B (1.2 to 1.4 ms at 1080p, level with the hexagon, and better everywhere); the separable hexagon for Tier A, since on a phone-sized frame gather's six passes cost twice its two; sprites for point highlights that must show the aperture; layers and tilt-shift with no depth; circular for none. Depth of field before bloom. Method-only cost on Dawn: `tests/dof-cost.test.ts`._

## Epic 4 — Bokeh scenes

Effects meant to be shipped, most of them Tier A because the orbs are generated, not blurred: a generated orb is drawn at its final size, which costs a fraction of blurring a frame.

- [x] **4.1 `bokeh-drift`** [A] (S) — the reference picture: orbs at several depths drifting in the dark, each the aperture's shape at its own circle of confusion, conserving its light, under one light in haze. ## Epic 5 — Moonlight

The moon as a light source seen through air, cloud, ice and water. Each is a known atmospheric optics phenomenon with a closed form or a small table.

- [x] **5.1 `moon-through-cloud`** [A] (M) — a bright moon behind thin moving cloud: the aureole, the cloud's silver edges, and the brightness falling where the cloud thickens. The light source for the rest of the epic. _`moon-through-cloud`, and the moon every Epic 5 effect shares (`src/wgsl/moon.wgsl`, twin `src/gpu/optics/moon.ts`, `tests/moon.test.ts`): 0.26° in radius, lit for its phase (waxing on the right), Lommel–Seeliger so a full moon is a flat coin, the maria where people expect them, faint earthshine. The cloud's optical depth at quarter size from sheared, drifting noise; the moon, sky and stars through e^−τ; scattered moonlight in proportion to τe^−τ, which silvers the thin edges and darkens the thick middles, times the moon's irradiance (its solid angle, 6.4 × 10⁻⁵ sr) and two shapes: a droplet-diffraction aureole peaking at about 1000 times isotropic, and a Henyey–Greenstein glow. Without the solid angle the whole frame went white: at 400 mm the view sits inside the forward peak. Exposure is a choice: the default glows as the eye sees it, "exposed for the face" is the photograph._
- [x] **5.2 `lunar-corona`** [A] (S) — the coloured rings of diffraction by cloud droplets, their radius set by droplet size, as a spectral sum of Airy patterns over a droplet-size spread (narrow spread for clear rings, wide for a plain aureole). _`lunar-corona`, on shared spectral tables (`src/gpu/optics/spectral.ts`, `profile.ts`, `corona.ts`; looked up by `src/wgsl/profile.wgsl`): Airy patterns over 32 wavelengths and a Gaussian spread of droplet radii, blurred by the moon's disc, built on the CPU only when the droplets change. For 10 µm droplets the aureole's red edge falls at 1.7° and the first full red ring at 3.2°, between the dark rings sin θ = 0.61 λ/a and 1.117 λ/a predict; ring size goes as 1/a; a narrow spread gives three red bands within 8°, a wide one none (`tests/lunar-corona.test.ts`). The cloud drifts, and a second noise varies the droplet size across it, so the rings wobble as it passes. Exposure is a photograph's: physically the rings are about a fiftieth of the aureole._
- [x] **5.3 `ice-halo`** [A] (S) — the 22° halo and moon dogs from hexagonal ice crystals, from the minimum-deviation profile, sharp red inner edge fading outwards. _`ice-halo`: a 3-D trace through a hexagonal column's six side faces with Fresnel losses (`halo.ts`). Minimum deviation 21.75° at 589 nm (21.6° red, 22.27° blue) with no traced ray below it, a red inner edge at 21.67° and the peak at 22.45° falling to 5% by 35°; moon dogs from level plates at the Bravais minimum, 22.2° from the moon at 10° elevation, none above about 61°. Columns only, side faces only: no 46° halo, parhelic circle or arcs._
- [x] **5.4 `moonlit-water`** [B] (M) — the glitter path of moonlight on a moving sea, built on Water Temple's `sun-glint`, with the focus pulled so the glints become a river of discs. _`moonlit-water`: Water Temple's swells ported (`swell.ts`) and its world-fixed glitter cells, each sending the Cox–Munk glitter of the shared moon's light, sized so every cell carries about the same expected light (with equal cells ten far ones held a third of the path and their twinkle moved it 6% a frame). Glinting cells become points at their distance through the point layer, so with the focus pulled to 1.5 m the path is a river of aperture-shaped discs; the moon itself is drawn as points too, the same disc out of focus. Each glint's brightness is a rising function of the capped flicker signal, so no glint can pass three flashes a second (tested over 300 seeds); the analyser reads `low` at every preset and under stress settings. The glint finder runs on the CPU (about 2 ms a frame for 6 000 cells): moving it to the GPU needs an indirect draw the point layer lacks. GPU time not measured._
- [x] **5.5 `lamp-in-fog`** [A] (S) — a street lamp in fog: 2.8's halo, the visible cone under it, and drifting density. Calm enough to sit behind text. _`lamp-in-fog`, and a shared cone of light in fog (`src/gpu/optics/beam.ts`, twin `src/wgsl/beam.wgsl`). 2.8's substitution carries over unchanged: a cone narrower than 90° only moves the integral's limits, so each ray's interval through it (a quadratic, lower nappe) becomes two angles at the lamp, and a Gauss–Legendre sum between them carries the shade's soft edge and the fog's density. With 12 points it is within 0.5% of a 200 000-step march over three cones, densities and phase asymmetries; the shader's 8 points within 3%. The halo is the bowl's 5% spill through the same sum, equal to `airlightForward` on an open ray. Drifting density from two fetches of Fire Temple's baked noise tile, now ported (`src/gpu/emission/noise-texture.ts`); ground pools, posts, faint wet-road glints, an optional moon, a capped breath. Flash `none`. One lamp read 1.96 ms in Chrome (just inside Tier A, GPU shared); a row of six, 3.78 ms: each lamp adds a halo sum to every pixel._
- [x] **5.6 Night vision** [—] (S) — a tail option for scenes lit at night: the Purkinje shift towards blue, scotopic loss of colour and acuity (Thompson, Shirley and Ferwerda). Decide whether every night scene should use it. _A tail option every effect has: `nightVision` off, moonlit (paper white as 0.1 cd/m²) or starlit (0.003), a pass before bloom (`src/gpu/post/night.ts`, `night.wgsl`). Thompson, Shirley and Ferwerda's model: scotopic luminance from XYZ, the cones' share by absolute luminance (all rods below 0.01 cd/m², all cones above about 4), a blue-grey for the rods, and a little lost acuity. By starlight red falls to under a quarter of its luminance and blue more than doubles (`tests/night.test.ts`, with the GPU pass against its twin). Decision: not on by default. It drains the coloured bokeh that is these effects' point, and lamps and the moon are bright enough for cones; it is for the dim surroundings of a scene, by preset._
- [x] **5.7 `moonbow`** [A] (S) — stretch goal: a faint, nearly white rainbow from moonlight on spray. _`moonbow`: a drop's geometric phase function for up to two internal reflections (`rainbow.ts`), 42.1° at 589 nm with red outside, the secondary at 50.9° reversed, Alexander's dark band 23 times darker than the sky inside the bow. A `colour` control desaturates it to 0.25 by default, as the eye sees a moonbow; 1 is a long exposure. No supernumeraries (geometric optics). Its own colour control overlaps with 5.6's night vision._

The image the stable way: a dropped image decoded with `createImageBitmap`, copied into an sRGB texture with `copyExternalImageToTexture` and mipmapped; the default is a dusk lake drawn on the GPU at load and developed like a JPEG, then taken down the same path. Drift through the capped flicker signal, the flare faded as sin² over its crossing, the held frame with leaks and no flare: the analyser reads `low` over 22 s at every preset. A page can only hand it an image by dropping one so far._
- [x] **6.10 `photo-focus`** [B] (M) — a photograph with a depth map: click to focus, with Epic 3's chosen method and real bokeh in the highlights. Ship one sample with its depth map; accept a dropped image and map. _Built. The sample is the depth-of-field test shot developed on the GPU at load as an 8-bit photo and disparity map, then refocused exactly as a dropped photo is: clipped highlights opened back up by a gain above a threshold, the map read as disparity between a near and far distance, and gather at the click's distance, eased like a focus motor. Drop a photo and its map together to use your own. Its size first showed gather's rings (3.2). 1.4 ms at 1080p on Dawn on the M1 Max (`docs/studies/epic-6.md`); Tier B._

## Epic 7 — Production readiness

What it takes to move an effect from demo to real site, kept short where the holofx backlog already plans it.

- [x] **7.1 Embed API** (S) — every effect usable as `mount(canvas, options)` from the library build, with per-effect gzip size reported. Mostly there from the shell. _`mount(canvas, effect, options)` from the library, on a shared GPU session made on first use (the old `mount(session, canvas, effect)` still works), and `adaptQuality` exported beside it. Per-effect download size of the library build: `npm run build:lib && npm run lib:sizes` → [docs/studies/library-sizes.md](docs/studies/library-sizes.md); 13 to 57 kB gzipped unminified, most of the bokeh family 16 to 18._
- [x] **7.2 Adaptive quality** (M) — a ladder per effect: fewer orbs, a cheaper depth-of-field method (3.9's order), lower glare resolution, then the CSS version. _`src/lib/quality.ts`: `adaptQuality(host, { budgetMs })` walks a ladder down after half a second over budget and back up after five seconds under 60% of it, restoring the page's own values. Each effect's rungs come first (fewer motes, facets or flakes; gather to the hexagon for `photo-focus`), then three-quarter resolution, no bloom, half resolution, then `onFallback` for the CSS version. Effects whose cost is the tail get no rungs of their own: fewer orbs would cost the look and save nothing (4.1). `tests/quality.test.ts`._
- [x] **7.3 Accessibility** (M) — flash over every preset, held frames that show the subject and no flare, the focus pull and parallax checked against WCAG 2.3.3 (animation from interactions) and switched off under reduced motion, and text contrast over bright moving bokeh with the scrim the contexts already provide. _[docs/accessibility.md](docs/accessibility.md): flash and held frames as before; every pointer and scroll motion stops under reduced motion because a paused host stops its loop, and every standalone page starts paused. Text contrast proven for any effect at once: text over an effect always sits on a plate, so the worst case is black or white under it, and `tests/contrast.test.ts` keeps AA for every style, plate and theme on SDR. On an opt-in HDR canvas a 3× highlight takes dark-theme muted text to 4.46._
- [x] **7.4 CSS fallbacks** (S) — for each Tier A effect, the best CSS version (radial gradients, blur, `mix-blend-mode`, a few keyframes) shown side by side, with its cost and what it cannot do. Bokeh is the family where CSS comes closest. _`src/fallbacks/`: a CSS disc-field generator (`mountCssFallback`, with a pure `planCssFallback` the tests read) animating only transform and opacity, held still under reduced motion, and refusing any config past its flash limits (twinkle period at least 1.5 s, dip at most 0.6, small twinkling lights only). A config for every Tier A effect (`CSS_FALLBACKS`): twelve honest fallbacks, six where the poster is better because the subject is what CSS cannot do (a moving focus, glitter, the moon's face), flare-cursor left out, and three `null` with reasons (aperture-lab, lens-light, and rack-focus-transition, whose CSS version is 6.8's page). `experiments/css-fallbacks.html` shows each beside its effect. Every CSS version held 60 frames a second; main thread is about 10 ms a second plus a third of a millisecond per animated element, so the dense fields (150 to 220 discs) cost 64 to 75 ms a second, about twice WebGPU bokeh-drift's 32: halve their disc counts before shipping ([docs/studies/css-fallbacks.md](docs/studies/css-fallbacks.md)). Not yet wired into the host's no-WebGPU path, which still shows the poster._
- [x] **7.5 Light pages** (S) — bokeh is added light, and a white page has no room for it. ## Epic 9 — Spectral light and glass

Light through a refracting solid, colour by colour. The reference for Epics 9 and 10 is a rotating wireframe icosahedron: sharp white edges over a glass body that glows cyan and blue inside, with hot spots of light near its corners, a horizon bent straight across its middle, and a soft halo that turns yellow, orange and finally red towards its rim. Read as optics that is four things: the backdrop refracted and dispersed through the glass (9.2 to 9.4), light gathered by internal reflection into caustics at the corners (9.5), the body out of focus behind a wire in focus (10.4), and a halo whose red spreads widest, as a lens's chromatic aberration and film's halation both make it (9.7, 10.2).

- [ ] **9.1 Spectral sampling** [—] (S) — light as wavelengths rather than three channels: wavelength to linear sRGB through Light Temple's `spectral_rgb` (or a CIE fit), and hero-wavelength sampling (Wilkie et al.), four wavelengths a sample spread over the visible range. Test: a flat spectrum integrates to white, and the colour of any single wavelength stays inside sRGB after the tonemap's clip.
- [ ] **9.2 Dispersive refraction** [—] (M) — index of refraction by wavelength (Cauchy for crown and flint glass, Sellmeier where Cauchy drifts; diamond for a stronger split), Snell's law, Fresnel reflectance and total internal reflection, and a ray tracer for convex polytopes as sets of planes (tetrahedron to icosahedron, prisms), several bounces inside. Test: a BK7 prism's minimum deviation at 589 nm and its spread from 486 to 656 nm against the textbook figures.
- [ ] **9.3 `prism`** [B] (M) — the classic: a beam of white light through a triangular prism opening into a spectrum on a wall behind it. The validation scene for 9.2, and a hero in its own right.
- [ ] **9.4 `glass-solid`** [B] (L) — a rotating glass Platonic solid (icosahedron by default) traced per pixel against a backdrop of sky, horizon and lights: refraction with dispersion, internal reflections to a set depth, Fresnel at every face, so edges split into spectra and the horizon bends through the body. Compare a fixed spectral sampling with three channels at three indices, the cheap version.
- [ ] **9.5 Caustics at the corners** [B] (M) — the bright spots where light inside the solid is reflected together near its vertices: traced from the light into a small caustic texture on the solid's faces, against a screen-space stand-in (a glow at each vertex weighted by how the faces around it face the light). Decide which the showpiece uses.
- [ ] **9.6 Thin-film colour** [A] (S) — soap-film iridescence on the faces, from Light Temple's thin-film chunk, as an option for a lighter, bubble-like solid.
- [ ] **9.7 Spectral depth of field** [B] (M) — longitudinal chromatic aberration: each wavelength focuses at a slightly different distance, so out of focus a bright object fringes one colour outside and its complement inside. The point layer and 3.9's chosen method with a circle of confusion per channel (three) against per wavelength (eight), checked against the 3.1 reference traced spectrally. This is the reference's red rim.

## Epic 10 — Glowing wireframes and halos

The other half of the reference: a sharp, bright line drawing of the solid, and the halo of light the whole thing throws.

- [ ] **10.1 HDR wireframe** [A] (S) — a solid's edges drawn with Storm Temple's stroke renderer (`src/gpu/strokes.ts`): constant width in pixels, energy per length so a long edge is not brighter than a short one, joints that do not double, hidden edges faded rather than removed, and a perspective that makes the solid read as turning.
- [ ] **10.2 Spectral bloom and halation** [B] (M) — bloom whose spread grows with wavelength, so a bright shape's halo goes white, yellow, orange and red outwards, as a real lens's scatter and film's halation both make it. Compared with three-channel bloom with a radius per channel, and with 2.5's halation; the colour must stay energy-conserving (white in, white integrated out).
- [ ] **10.3 Vertex flares** [A] (S) — a small starburst (2.6) where edges meet, brightest at the corners that face the light, capped by 2.9 so a turning solid never strobes.
- [ ] **10.4 Sharp wire over a soft body** [B] (M) — the reference's composition: the wire in focus in front, the glowing glass body behind or through it out of focus with its spectral fringes. Two layers, each with its own depth: the body through 9.7's spectral depth of field, the wire sharp over it. Decide whether the body is blurred as an image (3.9's method) or generated already soft.
- [ ] **10.5 `prism-icosahedron`** [C] (L) — the showpiece: a slowly rotating icosahedron, white wire edges and corner flares, a glass body that refracts and disperses the horizon behind it with caustic spots at its corners, all wrapped in a chromatic halo. The pointer turns it; reduced motion holds a still at a flattering angle. Built from 9.2, 9.4, 9.5, 9.7, 10.1 to 10.4, and judged against the reference picture side by side.
- [ ] **10.6 `wire-loader`** [A] (S) — the web-sized version: a small turning wireframe solid with its halo, as a loader or an icon, with no glass body (wire, flares and spectral bloom only) so it stays Tier A.
- [ ] **10.7 `glass-badge`** [B] (M) — the glass solid as a page element: a logo mark or a call-to-action extruded into a convex solid (the convex hull of its outline), refracting the page's own background image behind it. Uploads that image the stable way (6.1).
- [ ] **10.8 Study** [—] (M) — the cost of each piece at 1080p and phone size (traced glass, caustics, spectral bloom), which ones a Tier B version can keep, and flash: a bright solid turning in front of a light is a large area changing brightness, and the analyser decides how fast it may turn.

## Risks and open questions

- **CSS is a strong rival.** Soft circles drifting on a dark page are a few radial gradients and keyframes. A shader has to earn its place with things CSS cannot do: aperture shape, energy conservation as orbs overlap, real depth of field, thousands of points, focus that follows input. Stories 7.4 and 8.4 measure this directly, and some Tier A effects may end with "use the CSS".
- **Shimmer.** Highlights are single bright pixels before they are defocused, and they crawl and flicker under motion. Point sources with exact coverage (2.3), the Karis average and temporal stability (3.7) are requirements, not polish.
- **Flash.** Glints, glitter, twinkling lights and flares crossing the view all sit near WCAG 2.3.1. The flicker caps (2.9) and the analyser are in from the start.
- **Motion.** A focus pull and parallax on scroll can trouble visitors with vestibular disorders (WCAG 2.3.3). Every scroll-linked effect needs a still version.
- **Light pages.** Like fire, bokeh is light. Some effects will honestly be dark-section only (7.5).
- **No depth for the page.** Depth of field over DOM content is limited to layers and CSS blur. `photo-focus` needs a depth map the visitor will not have; the sample ships one.
- **Scatter cost.** Sprite bokeh and the reference scatter grow with the number of bright pixels and the disc area; the worst case (a night street fully defocused) may be over Tier B on an integrated GPU.
- **Overlap.** Several neighbours already exist or are planned (table above). The value here is the optics and the comparisons, not more orb backgrounds.
- **Measurement.** The same as every temple: one machine, a 1 ms browser floor, and effects under about 2 ms that cannot be ranked. The tiers stay provisional until 8.2.
- **Spectral cost.** Tracing a glass solid per pixel with several wavelengths and bounces is a Tier C budget at full resolution; the web-sized versions (10.6, 10.7) have to drop the glass or trace it at reduced resolution.
- **Open questions.** Is a generated orb field ever beaten by blurring a sharp render? Does a baked aperture kernel beat the analytic shape on a phone? Does depth of field belong before or after bloom (3.9)? How many glints a second can a field hold before the analyser objects?

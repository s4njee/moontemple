EXPERIMENT 06 / A MOON BEHIND CLOUD

Moonlight.

The moon through a 400 mm lens, waxing and waning a month a minute, behind thin cloud drifting slowly across it: a soft glow round the moon, silver edges where the cloud is thin and lit, and the light falling away where it thickens, all of it tinged a little blue, the way night is painted and filmed.

WHERE THE GLOW COMES FROM

One moon,
a little cloud.

01 / AUREOLE

A ring of glow.

The droplets in thin cloud scatter moonlight mostly forwards, a few degrees off its path, so the moon sits in a soft glow that fades with angle. The thinner the cloud, the tighter and brighter the glow.

02 / EDGES

Silver linings.

Cloud nearest the moon is lit brightest from behind, and its thin edges let the most light through, so they shine. Where the cloud thickens it swallows the light, and the moon goes dim behind it.

03 / TINT

Blue by night.

Moonlight is sunlight off grey rock, a shade warmer than daylight, but night eyes see it blue: by moonlight the rods take over, and they favour blue. So night is painted and filmed blue, and this page does the same. Switch the tint off for the moon as a camera records it.

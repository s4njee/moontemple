EXPERIMENT 04 / A MEADOW AT DUSK

Fireflies.

A hundred and eighty fireflies over the grass, each glowing on its own slow rhythm, through a 50 mm lens at f/1.4 focused two and a half metres out. The few near the focus are sharp points; the rest are soft discs. Move the pointer down the page to move the focus out through them.

How it works ↓
THE PAGE IN FRONT / THE MEADOW BEHIND IT04 — A 50 MM LENS AT F/1.4

WHERE THE GLOW COMES FROM

Small lights,
slowly breathing.

01 / FOCUS

Moved by the pointer.

The top of the page focuses half a metre away, the bottom fourteen metres across the meadow, and the lens eases between them. A firefly in the plane of focus is a point; one nearer or farther is a disc as wide as its blur.

02 / GLOW

Each on its own rhythm.

Every firefly swells and fades once every few seconds, a third of a second at its brightest, and glows faintly between. No two share a period, so the swarm never pulses together and never flashes.

03 / DEPTH

Wandering in depth.

They drift slowly in every direction, towards the lens and away from it as well as across, so a disc swells as its firefly comes closer and shrinks to a point as it passes through the focus.

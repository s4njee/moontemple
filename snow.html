EXPERIMENT 02 / A LAMP IN A BLIZZARD

Nightsnow.

Four thousand flakes driven sideways past a street lamp, slowed right down, through a long lens focused seven metres out. The flakes near the lamp catch its light, the far ones are specks in the fog, and the few that blow close to the lens are wide, faint discs.

How it works ↓
THE PAGE IN FRONT / THE STORM BEHIND IT02 — AN 85 MM LENS AT F/1.8

WHERE THE LIGHT COMES FROM

One lamp,
a lot of snow.

01 / DEPTH

Blurred by distance.

The flakes fill the view from half a metre to thirty metres out. Only those near the focus distance are sharp: nearer ones swell into discs as wide as the lens is open, farther ones shrink towards points.

02 / LIGHT

Lit from behind.

Each flake is lit by the lamp, falling off with the square of the distance, and scatters mostly forwards. So the flakes between you and the lamp shine, and the ones off to the side are barely there.

03 / FOG

Thick air.

The blizzard's fog dims every flake by its distance and spreads the lamp into a halo, worked out in closed form for each pixel. Change the focus and the discs and the specks trade places.
